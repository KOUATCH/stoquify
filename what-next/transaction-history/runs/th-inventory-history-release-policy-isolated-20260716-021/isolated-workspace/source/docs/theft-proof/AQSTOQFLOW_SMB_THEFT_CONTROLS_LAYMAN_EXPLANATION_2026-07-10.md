## Simple Introduction

This document explains how AqStoqFlow protects a business from common theft, leakage, and abuse.

The system does not claim that theft becomes physically impossible. Software cannot stop a person from touching cash, carrying goods, lying, or trying to cheat.

What the system can do is make theft much harder to hide, easier to detect, faster to investigate, and riskier for dishonest behavior.

The main idea is simple:

> Every important business action should leave evidence.

If stock moves, the system should know why.

If cash is collected, the system should show where it came from.

If a supplier is paid, the system should show the approved invoice and payment evidence.

If a staff member changes prices, gives a discount, writes off stock, or approves a refund, the system should record who did it, when they did it, and why.

## Important Terms In Plain Language

### Theft-Resistant

This means the system reduces the opportunity to steal and reduces the chance that theft can be hidden.

Example: A cashier can still try to pocket money, but the system compares sales, receipts, cash drawer count, and mobile-money collections. If something does not match, the owner sees an alert.

### Evidence-Controlled

This means sensitive actions must be supported by proof.

Example: If an employee says goods were damaged, the system can require a reason, approval, and supporting evidence before stock is removed from the books.

### Audit Trail

An audit trail is a history of who did what in the system.

Example: If a supplier payment account is changed, the system records the user, date, old value, new value, and approval status.

### Maker-Checker

Maker-checker means one person starts an action and another person approves it.

Example: The person who creates a supplier payment should not be the same person who approves that payment.

### Reconciliation

Reconciliation means comparing two records that should match.

Example: Sales recorded in the POS should match cash counted in the drawer and mobile-money settlements received.

### Exception Dashboard

An exception dashboard shows only things that need attention.

Example: Instead of showing the owner thousands of sales lines, it shows "cash drawer shortage," "unapproved stock adjustment," "supplier invoice duplicate," or "high refund by same cashier."

## Why Theft Hurts The Business

Theft does not only remove money or stock. It weakens the whole business.

When theft is not controlled, the owner may see:

- cash shortages
- missing stock
- wrong profit reports
- delayed supplier payments
- customer complaints
- staff blaming each other
- accountant unable to close the books
- tax and compliance risk
- owner forced to personally supervise everything

A good control system improves efficiency because it tells the owner:

- what happened
- who did it
- when it happened
- what evidence exists
- what still needs approval
- what needs investigation

## Main Areas Protected

### Inventory

The system protects stock by recording every movement, comparing expected stock to physical count, requiring approval for adjustments, and showing repeated shortage patterns.

Positive impact: stock becomes more accurate, stockouts reduce, and fake write-offs become harder to hide.

### Cash Sales

The system protects cash sales through receipt numbers, shift close, cash drawer counts, and sales-to-cash reconciliation.

Positive impact: the owner can see whether recorded sales match actual cash collected.

### Refunds, Voids, And Discounts

The system protects these actions by requiring original sale references, reasons, permissions, and approvals for risky cases.

Positive impact: staff cannot easily use refunds, voids, or discounts as quiet cash-leakage tools.

### Purchasing And Suppliers

The system protects purchasing by matching purchase orders, goods received, supplier invoices, and payments.

Positive impact: the business pays only for approved goods that were actually received.

### Supplier Payments

The system protects payments by requiring approval, evidence, duplicate checks, and controlled changes to supplier bank or mobile-money details.

Positive impact: money is less likely to be diverted to the wrong account.

### Customer Collections

The system protects customer payments by issuing receipts, updating customer ledgers, and reconciling collections to cash, bank, or mobile-money deposits.

Positive impact: customer balances become more trustworthy.

### Payroll

The system protects payroll by approving employee records, reviewing payroll registers, linking attendance to pay, and reconciling payroll payments.

Positive impact: ghost workers, fake overtime, and payment redirection become harder.

### Expenses And Advances

The system protects small cash movements by requiring receipts, approvals, duplicate checks, petty cash ledgers, and advance retirement tracking.

Positive impact: small recurring leakage becomes visible.

### Mobile Money And Bank Settlement

The system protects payment channels by matching recorded sales to bank and mobile-money settlement records.

Positive impact: the owner sees what was sold and what money actually arrived.

### Master Data And Access

The system protects sensitive setup such as prices, units, supplier details, tax codes, accounts, and user roles.

Positive impact: fraud cannot easily hide inside system settings.

## Core Controls Explained Simply

### Service-Owned Truth

Important records should come from trusted system workflows, not silent manual edits.

### Immutable Ledgers

The system keeps history. If something is corrected, the correction is recorded instead of deleting the past.

### Maker-Checker Approval

One person creates the action. Another person approves it.

### Evidence Requirements

Sensitive actions need proof, such as receipts, count sheets, photos, delivery notes, payment confirmations, or document hashes.

### Reconciliation

The system compares records that should match.

Examples:

- sales versus cash
- sales versus mobile money
- purchase order versus goods received versus invoice
- stock sent versus stock received
- payroll register versus payment batch

### Owner Alerts

The owner should see exceptions clearly, not only technical reports.

The system protects against missing stock, stolen cash, fake refunds, inflated supplier invoices, duplicate payments, ghost payroll, fake expenses, mobile-money leakage, unauthorized setup changes, shared-password abuse, and weak accounting evidence.

The main controls are ledgers, physical counts, approvals, audit trails, evidence requirements, reconciliations, user permissions, maker-checker rules, dashboards, alerts, and period locks.

Owners benefit through better visibility, stronger staff accountability, better stock accuracy, safer cash control, cleaner accounting, faster investigation, and more confidence in the business.
