1. EXECUTION CHECKLIST

A. Establish scope and evidence baseline
- Read applicable AGENTS.md instructions and package scripts.
- Record the current commit, audit timestamp, dirty-worktree state, and relevant file fingerprints. Preserve existing changes.
- Identify supported customer segments, countries, currencies, organization structures, personas, modules, and product promises.
- Distinguish confirmed requirements from assumptions and proposed capabilities.
- Inventory schema models, services, routes, actions, jobs, integration adapters, tests, feature flags, and entitlement boundaries.
- Use existing graph data and reports for navigation. Check freshness and verify important relationships against current source.
- Maintain a coverage register. Never silently omit a module because it is complex or lacks tests.

B. Inspect the database design
- Review the complete Prisma schema, SQL migration history, custom constraints, indexes, triggers, views, and relevant seed/backfill logic.
- Map every model to its business purpose, owning domain, tenant boundary, authoritative writer, lifecycle, and retention policy.
- Trace relations and references across domains, including identifiers stored without foreign keys and critical data stored in JSON.
- Examine cardinality, nullability, uniqueness, tenant-consistent foreign keys, deletion behavior, document numbering, and historical evidence preservation.
- Evaluate monetary and quantity precision, rounding, currency denomination, exchange-rate provenance, units, dates, time zones, effective dates, and accounting cutoffs.
- Identify stored derived values and prove how they stay consistent with their source records.
- Examine transaction isolation, locking, compare-and-set/version checks, idempotency, outbox atomicity, and retry behavior.
- Assess query patterns, tenant-leading indexes, pagination, unbounded exports, and aggregation performance using evidence.
- Evaluate fresh migration replay, upgrade paths, backfills, restore procedures, and rollback feasibility.
- Separate intended schema, migration-defined schema, and deployed schema. Inspect deployed metadata only through an available authorized read-only connection; otherwise mark drift and runtime enforcement unverified.
- Do not classify a missing ORM constraint as a confirmed defect until database and application enforcement have been examined.

C. Trace complete business journeys
For each journey, follow:
user intent → authorization/entitlement → action/API → owning service → transaction and persistence → event/outbox → downstream effects → UI state → report/export → audit evidence.

Inspect happy paths, denial paths, partial success, duplicate commands, stale data, concurrent commands, retries, corrections, and recovery.

At minimum, trace:
- Online sale → stock/COGS → tender or receivable → settlement → reconciliation → ledger → reporting.
- Offline sale → local persistence → restart/reconnection → replay → conflict resolution → stock/payment/fiscal/ledger effects.
- Purchase order → partial receipt → supplier invoice → matching → approval → payment → reconciliation → AP/ledger.
- Return/refund/credit note → stock disposition → payment or balance correction → accounting reversal.
- Employee onboarding → effective contract/compensation → attendance/leave → payroll → approval → payslip → payment/declaration → accounting.
- Period close → unresolved-source checks → reconciliation/sign-off → locking → reports → subsequent corrections and evidence invalidation.

D. Verify findings and produce the remediation plan
- Reproduce significant findings where feasible using focused tests or isolated fixtures.
- Distinguish static inspection, mocked tests, actual database tests, browser evidence, and deployed evidence.
- Revalidate historical findings; classify each as confirmed, resolved, changed, or unverified.
- Keep scanner defects, missing evidence, missing configuration, missing qualified approval, and implementation defects separate.
- Prioritize corrections by business impact and dependency order.

2. DOMAIN COVERAGE

Offline and POS
Assess the actual offline capability boundary for each workflow. Cover device registration, local durability, restart recovery, tenant/session changes, cached-data protection, sequence/version handling, clock skew, duplicate/reordered delivery, payload integrity, authorization freshness, conflicts, rejected-event recovery, and sync observability.

Prove that replay cannot duplicate stock, money, receivables, fiscal documents, or journals. Verify how provisional receipts and unconfirmed electronic payments are represented.

Sales and customers
Cover quotations/orders where supported, fulfillment, pricing, discounts, tax, split and partial payments, credit sales, deposits, customer balances, allocations, returns, refunds, voids, receipts, cash sessions, shortages, and end-of-day close.

Check that sale, payment, fulfillment, and accounting statuses have distinct, consistent meanings.

Inventory
Cover units/conversions, locations, reservations, transfers, counts, adjustments, losses, returns, lots/serials/expiry where applicable, negative-stock policy, backdated movements, valuation, and COGS.

Reconcile movement history, quantities, valuation layers or cost calculations, and ledger balances. Test competing sales and transfers.

Purchasing and AP
Cover supplier lifecycle, purchasing approvals, partial receipts, invoice capture, duplicate detection, two/three-way matching, tolerances, returns, credit notes, landed costs, supplier balances, payment allocation, and correction workflows.

Evaluate maker-checker controls and changes to supplier payment destinations.

Payments and treasury
Separate payment intent, provider confirmation, settlement, allocation, refund, and accounting recognition.

Inspect cash, bank, card, and mobile-money paths actually supported. Cover provider signatures, callbacks, replay protection, polling, timeouts, uncertain outcomes, fees, reversals, duplicate references, and settlement timing.

Reconciliation
Assess bank/provider import provenance, deduplication, matching rules, partial/many-to-many matches, gross-versus-net treatment, fees, suspense, exception ownership, manual overrides, approvals, sign-off, and stale evidence.

Require every resolved discrepancy to retain its source evidence and resulting financial effects.

Professional accounting
Evaluate chart of accounts, journals, posting rules, source links, balanced entries, subledgers, receivables/payables, opening balances, accruals, expenses, fixed assets/depreciation where supported, taxes, foreign currencies, period locks, reversals, year-end closing, and carry-forward.

Inspect general ledger, trial balance, balance sheet, income statement, cash-flow reporting, notes, accountant access, and exports.

Verify source completeness and amounts independently: a balanced journal or trial balance alone does not establish correctness.

Recheck earlier audit concerns involving currency semantics, source-owned reversals, close concurrency, reporting completeness, and misleading assurance labels without assuming those concerns remain unresolved.

HRIS
Cover employee identity, organization structure, branch/manager scope, employment assignments, effective-dated contracts, compensation approvals, documents, attendance, leave, onboarding/offboarding, privacy, retention, and employee self-service.

Check that historical payroll remains reproducible after employee data changes.

Payroll
Cover input readiness, immutable snapshots, versioned rules, earnings/deductions, employer charges, rounding, prorations, retroactivity, corrections, approval separation, payslips, payment batches, failures, employee balances, declarations, and accounting tie-outs.

Assess support separately for each country and rule version. Do not infer uniform tax or labor rules from OHADA membership.

Country packs and compliance
Identify declared jurisdictions and actual supported capabilities. Check effective dates, official sources, reviewed rule provenance, fiscal numbering, filing/export formats, and supported versus draft/disabled states.

Use current authoritative sources for substantive legal, tax, payroll, and accounting requirements. Record retrieval dates and where qualified review is still required.

Platform controls and operations
Cover tenancy, RBAC, entitlements, fresh authentication, segregation of duties, imports/exports, audit trails, redaction, secrets, error handling, worker scheduling, retries, dead letters, monitoring, backups/restores, recovery objectives, and deployment/migration safety.

Inspect AI and automation authority, evidence grounding, injection resistance, approvals, evaluation, and cost controls where those features exist.

UX and workflow completeness
Check role-based navigation, discoverability, dense forms/tables, English/French content, accessibility, mobile use, and loading/empty/error/stale/offline/denied states.

Confirm that users can understand blocked actions and complete recovery without hidden operational intervention.
