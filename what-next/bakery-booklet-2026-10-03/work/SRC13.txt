 Commercial Bakery Strategy and Recipe Manual
Equipment utilization, corrected production mathematics, product development, quality, and distribution
Prepared from six bakery PDFs downloaded September 11, 2026
Working context: Cameroon, with Nkambe, Douala, and Yaoundé references where supported by the source material
Purpose. This manual converts the supplied bakery documents into a commercially usable operating system. It distinguishes
source information from corrections, assumptions, and new recommendations. The objective is dependable product quality,
profitable use of the available equipment, disciplined food safety, and a defensible route to market.
Executive decision. Launch with a focused portfolio of staple bread, a few high-margin bakery products, one signature lunch
platform, and a small cold line. Build scale only after actual tray capacity, dough yield, oven recovery, proofing capacity, costs,
and customer demand have been measured.
1 Source review and professional audit
Six PDFs were reviewed: Commercial Baguette Production Guide; Staggered Baguette Production Schedule; Cameroon Bakery
Empire Master Guide; Bakery Empire Master Operations Manual; Nkambe Bakery Empire Master Operations Manual; and
Nkambe Bakery Empire Master Operations and Formulation Manual. Together they contain baguette controls, a five-batch shift
model, 24 product concepts, merchandising, tricycle logistics, and basic equipment guidance.
The documents are useful as a starting blueprint, but they contain repeated assumptions and some unsafe or unverified
instructions. The following corrections should be treated as release gates before production claims are made.

2 Business model and competitive position
The strongest model is a three-engine bakery. Engine one is dependable bread volume. Engine two is high-margin pastries,
biscuits, cakes, and filled snacks. Engine three is lunch and afternoon traffic through shawarma, fries, soft serve, and popsicles.
This combination keeps the oven productive in the morning, uses the food-service equipment at midday, and creates a second
sales peak when bread demand slows.

Do not launch all 24 source products at once. A broad menu creates ingredient complexity, changeover losses, inconsistent
training, and unsold stock. Start with eight to ten products, then add items only when a product has repeat demand and a positive
contribution per bottleneck minute.
• Defensible advantages: exact weights, clean and attractive retail presentation, dependable delivery, uniform buns for food
 vendors, transparent oil management, and local signature flavors.
• Avoid unverified claims about competitors, shelf life, nutrition, or market share. Demonstrate superiority through measured
 weight, delivery reliability, customer feedback, and repeat orders.
• Use product architecture: value staples, reliable mid-tier products, and premium signature items. Do not make every item
 premium; protect affordability on the daily bread line.

4 Corrected baguette production mathematics
Use baker’s percentages and a weighed batch sheet. For the 80-loaf configuration, the 14.2 kg flour formula in the source guide is
operationally coherent: 14.2 kg flour, 9.37 kg water, 284 g salt, and 71 g instant yeast produce approximately 23.93 kg dough, or
79.8 pieces at 300 g. It is the correct starting batch if the physical rack holds five pieces on each of 16 trays.

Useful water-temperature formula: target dough temperature × 3 minus flour temperature, room temperature, and measured mixer
friction factor. Establish the friction factor with three test batches. Do not blindly add ice; excessive ice can delay yeast activity and
create uneven mixing.
The source schedule is best used as a starting rhythm: mix batches 45 minutes apart, keep proofing racks within capacity, and
bake only when the rack is ready. The 45-minute rhythm must be changed if the actual oven or proofer cannot support it.

6 New strategic recipes
The following recipes are new additions to the reviewed source portfolio. They include the six requested products plus three
additional high-potential products and complementary formats. They are starting formulas, not final commercial specifications.
Validate ingredients, allergen declarations, cooking limits, packaging, and shelf life locally before sale.
6.1 Signature chicken shawarma baguette
Formula. Marinade for 10 kg chicken: chicken thigh 10 kg; plain yogurt 800 g; oil 300 g; onion purée 500 g; lime or lemon juice
250 g; garlic 150 g; paprika 100 g; ginger 80 g; cumin and coriander 60 g; black pepper 30 g; salt 180 g; optional chili 30 g. Target
sandwich: 250 g baguette, 80–100 g cooked chicken, 100–120 g fries, 30 g sauce, and 30–40 g salad.
Manufacturing process. Blend marinade in a sanitized stick blender, mix in the tank, add chicken, cover and refrigerate for 4–12
hours. Load the shawarma machine. Slice only the cooked outside surface and continue cooking the remaining cylinder. Check
poultry using a calibrated thermometer and a validated internal cooking limit. Assemble to a fixed portion weight. Never reuse raw
marinade as sauce.
Quality controls: cooked chicken weight, sandwich assembly time, bread integrity, oil color, sauce temperature, and customer wait
time. Failure points are undercooked meat, warm sauce, soggy bread, uncontrolled portions, and raw-meat cross-contamination.
Sell as a signature lunch combo with a drink or popsicle.
6.2 Cocoa and groundnut breakfast loaf
Formula. For an 18 kg flour-equivalent base: bread flour 17.1 kg; roasted groundnut flour 900 g; cocoa powder 540 g; water or
milk 10.8 kg; sugar 1.44 kg; vegetable oil 900 g; milk powder 540 g; salt 324 g; instant yeast 180 g; optional chocolate chips 1.8
kg.
Manufacturing process. Mix dry ingredients with liquid and rest 15 minutes. Add sugar, oil, salt, and yeast. Mix to medium gluten
development. Add chips during the final minute. Bulk ferment 45 minutes, scale 250–300 g, proof 45–60 minutes, and bake near
190°C for 18–22 minutes.

Quality controls: even cocoa color, nut aroma without rancidity, loaf weight, crumb softness, and allergen label. Do not make
nutrition claims without analysis. Groundnut is a major allergen; use dedicated storage and truthful labeling. Position as a local

6.3 Chicken and vegetable filled bakery pocket
Formula. Dough: flour 10 kg; water 5.5 kg; sugar 500 g; oil 400 g; salt 180 g; instant yeast 100 g. Filling: cooked shredded chicken
5 kg; cabbage 1 kg; carrot 800 g; onion 800 g; oil 250 g; seasoning 100 g; breadcrumbs or cooked potato binder 500 g.
Manufacturing process. Mix dough and rest 30–45 minutes. Divide 60–70 g, roll, fill with approximately 40 g, seal, egg-wash, and
bake at 195–205°C for 15–18 minutes. Cook and cool the filling under controlled conditions before assembly. Keep raw and
cooked chicken physically separate.
Quality controls: seal strength, filling weight, no leakage, bake color, and cooled pack temperature. Cost savings come from
trimming vegetables into the filling and using measured portions. Target schools, offices, kiosks, and commuter traffic.
6.4 Mango-ginger soft serve
Formula. For approximately 10 kg: milk 5.5 kg; cream 1.2 kg; sugar 1.4 kg; skim milk powder 600 g; dextrose or glucose 250 g;
mango purée 1 kg; lime juice 50 g; stabilizer approximately 50 g according to supplier instructions.
Manufacturing process. Combine dairy and dry ingredients. Apply a validated pasteurization process, cool rapidly to 4°C, age
under refrigeration, blend in treated mango purée, and load the soft-serve machine according to its manual. Record mix
temperature, draw temperature, and cleaning completion.
Quality controls: sweetness, fruit color, draw consistency, mix temperature, and machine sanitation. Use a refractometer to
reduce batch variation. Do not sell a dairy product with an unvalidated shelf life or poor cold-chain records.
6.5 Bissap, ginger, and lime popsicle
Formula. For approximately 10 kg: filtered water 6.5 kg; strong filtered hibiscus infusion 1.5 kg; 65°Brix sugar syrup 1.8 kg; ginger
juice 150 g; lime juice 50 g; stabilizer 20–30 g according to supplier instructions.
Manufacturing process. Prepare and filter the infusion. Combine in the stirred tank, dissolve syrup fully, and adjust final
sweetness. A starting range of approximately 18–22°Brix may be tested. Fill molds, insert sticks, freeze rapidly, demold, wrap,
and store frozen.
Quality controls: Brix, acidity, color, stick position, demolding losses, package seal, and frozen storage temperature. This is a
low-cost local signature product suited to children, families, and afternoon retail traffic.
6.6 Samosa: fried or oven-baked
Formula. Wrapper for 10 kg flour: flour 10 kg; water 4.2 kg; oil 300 g; salt 180 g; optional cumin or ajwain 20 g. Filling: cooked
potato 4 kg; cooked peas 1 kg; onion 800 g; oil 300 g; ginger and garlic 150 g; curry spice blend 120 g; salt 100 g. For chicken or
beef, replace part of the potato with fully cooked, drained meat.
Manufacturing process. Mix a stiff dough just until smooth, cover and rest 30 minutes. Divide into 25–30 g pieces, roll thin, cut or
fold into wrappers, fill with 30–35 g cooled filling, and seal with a flour-water paste. Fry in clean oil at 170–180°C until crisp, or
bake at 200°C for approximately 18–22 minutes after brushing lightly with oil. Keep filling cold enough for safe handling and dry
enough to prevent bursting.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check wrapper thickness, sealed edges, filling weight, oil temperature, and crispness
after cooling. Over-wet filling causes leaks; over-hot oil burns the wrapper before the filling warms. Offer potato-pea as the value
SKU and chicken or beef as the premium SKU for kiosks, schools, and events.
6.7 Soft shawarma wrap flatbread
Formula. For 10 kg flour: flour 10 kg; water 5.8 kg; vegetable oil 500 g; sugar 100 g; salt 180 g; instant yeast 80 g. Filling per wrap:
80–100 g cooked shawarma meat, 25–30 g sauce, 30–40 g salad, and optional 60–80 g fries.
Manufacturing process. Mix to a soft, extensible dough. Bulk-rest 30–45 minutes, divide into 80–90 g pieces, round, rest 10
minutes, and roll to approximately 24–26 cm. Cook on a clean hot griddle or hot oven tray until flexible but not dry. Stack under a
covered cloth. Assemble with fixed portions, fold tightly, wrap in food-grade paper, and serve promptly.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. The flatbread must bend without cracking and must not be wet from sauce. Use the
shawarma machine for the meat and the 50 L tank for marinades and sauces. Sell a standard wrap, a double-meat premium
wrap, and a family platter.
6.8 Yeasted ring donuts
Formula. For 10 kg flour: milk or water 5 kg; sugar 1.2 kg; whole egg 1 kg; margarine or butter 1 kg; salt 180 g; instant yeast 150
g; vanilla 50 g. Use flour with enough strength to hold shape but avoid excessive mixing.
Manufacturing process. Mix liquid, yeast, sugar, flour, and egg on low speed. Add salt and fat after the dough forms; mix to a
smooth, moderately developed dough. Bulk-ferment 45–60 minutes. Sheet to approximately 12 mm, cut 55–65 g rings, proof at
28–30°C and 70–80% RH for 30–45 minutes, then fry at 175–180°C for approximately 60–90 seconds per side. Drain on racks,
not directly on absorbent paper for long periods.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check ring symmetry, proof level, oil temperature, internal cook, and oil absorption.
Underproofed donuts split or become dense; overproofed donuts collapse. Use a fixed piece weight and offer plain sugar,

6.9 Glazed donuts
Formula. Use the yeasted ring donut dough above. Glaze per 1 kg icing sugar: 180–220 g warm water or milk, 20 g glucose or
corn syrup, 5 g vanilla, and a small amount of lemon juice if desired. Adjust viscosity so the glaze coats without running off.
Manufacturing process. Fry and cool donuts until warm but not hot. Dip the top once, allow excess glaze to drain, and place on a
clean rack for 10–15 minutes. Add cocoa, coffee, coconut, or colored local-fruit variations only after the base glaze is stable.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Control glaze temperature, viscosity, coverage, shine, and drying time. Glaze only
clean, dry donuts. Package in single units or ventilated boxes to prevent condensation. This is a strong breakfast and impulse
product for retail and office delivery.
6.10 Beignets soufflés
Formula. For 10 kg flour: water or milk 5.5 kg; sugar 1.5 kg; whole egg 1.5 kg; oil or butter 1 kg; instant yeast 200 g; salt 180 g;
vanilla or nutmeg 30 g. Target small 20–25 g pieces.
Manufacturing process. Mix to a soft, elastic dough. Bulk-ferment 60–90 minutes until visibly expanded. Portion with a lightly oiled
scoop or hand, keeping pieces similar in size. Fry at 170–175°C until puffed and golden, turning as required. Drain, cool briefly,
and toss in fine sugar or serve plain.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check puff, light interior, oil absorption, and uniformity. Dough that is too stiff
produces heavy balls; oil that is too hot creates a dark shell and raw center. Sell fresh in small paper bags during breakfast and
school traffic.
6.11 Beignets sucrés
Formula. For 10 kg flour: milk or water 4.8 kg; sugar 1.5 kg; whole egg 1.2 kg; butter or margarine 1 kg; instant yeast 150 g; salt
180 g; vanilla 40 g. Optional lemon zest 20 g.
Manufacturing process. Mix to a smooth sweet dough and bulk-ferment 45–60 minutes. Sheet to 8–10 mm, cut 35–45 g squares
or rounds, rest 15–20 minutes, and fry at 170–175°C until evenly golden. Drain, then coat with granulated sugar, cinnamon sugar,
or a light icing sugar dusting.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Control thickness, fry temperature, sugar adhesion, and oil freshness. Use a smaller
piece for value packs and a filled or flavored version for premium retail. Do not confuse this product with the lighter,
hand-portioned beignet soufflé.
6.12 Savory meat pie
Formula. Pastry for 10 kg flour: flour 10 kg; margarine or shortening 4 kg; cold water 3.5 kg; salt 150 g; sugar 100 g. Filling:
cooked minced beef or chicken 6 kg; potato 3 kg; carrot 2 kg; onion 1 kg; oil 250 g; stock or thickened gravy as required;
seasoning 120 g.
Manufacturing process. Rub fat into flour, add cold water and salt, and mix only until the pastry comes together. Rest chilled for 30
minutes. Prepare a cooked, thick filling and cool it before assembly. Roll pastry, cut rounds, fill with 60–80 g, seal, egg-wash, vent,
and bake at 190–200°C for 22–30 minutes.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check pastry flakiness, seal, filling weight, leakage, and internal cooking. Keep the
filling thick and cold before assembly. This is a strong school, office, kiosk, and catering product that uses the oven without
consuming fryer capacity.
6.13 Cinnamon rolls
Formula. Dough for 10 kg flour: milk or water 5 kg; sugar 1 kg; eggs 1 kg; butter or margarine 1.5 kg; salt 150 g; instant yeast 150
g. Filling: butter 1.2 kg; brown or white sugar 2 kg; cinnamon 100 g. Optional glaze: icing sugar with a small amount of milk.
Manufacturing process. Mix dough to medium development, bulk-ferment 45–60 minutes, roll into a rectangle, spread filling
evenly, roll tightly, and cut into 80–100 g pieces. Place with expansion space, proof 30–45 minutes, and bake at 175–185°C for
18–22 minutes. Glaze after cooling slightly.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check spiral definition, proof, center bake, filling leakage, and glaze coverage. Use
the same dough for cardamom, cocoa, coconut, or pineapple-ginger variations. Position as a premium breakfast pastry for coffee
bundles.
6.14 Mini pizza and vendor flatbread
Formula. For 10 kg flour: water 6 kg; oil 500 g; sugar 200 g; salt 180 g; instant yeast 100 g. Topping base per batch: tomato sauce
4 kg; cheese 3 kg; vegetables or cooked chicken 4 kg; herbs and spices as required.
Manufacturing process. Mix and bulk-ferment 45–60 minutes. Divide into 100–120 g pieces, round, rest, and flatten. Apply
measured sauce and topping, leaving a clean edge. Bake at 215–225°C for 8–12 minutes until the base is set and cheese is
melted. Use cooked, cooled meat only and avoid excessive wet toppings.
Quality, commercial use, and failure control. Check base thickness, topping weight, bottom bake, cheese melt, and holding time.
Sell individual mini pizzas at retail and provide larger vendor or event trays to improve oven utilization.

7 Existing recipe portfolio from the source PDFs
The source documents provide 24 recipe concepts. The summaries below preserve their main formulations and operating intent
while adding the most important validation caution. Scale all formulas proportionally from the stated flour base and confirm actual
yield.
1. French baguette
Formula. 14.2 kg flour; 9.37 kg ice water; 284 g salt; 71 g instant yeast, or the larger 18 kg flour version with 10.38 kg water plus
1.50 kg ice.
Process and control. Mix 3 min low, rest/autolyse 10 min, mix 5 min high; bulk 45 min; scale around 294–300 g; bench rest; mold;
proof near 26°C; steam immediately; bake near 220°C. Validate rack capacity and finished weight.
2. Premium Kumba bread
Formula. 18 kg flour; water approximately 9.0–9.9 kg; sugar 2.16 kg; fat 1.08–1.44 kg; salt 270–360 g; yeast 180 g; nutmeg 36–54
g.
Process and control. Mix to a dense uniform dough, mold tightly, proof approximately 75–120 min, bake near 190°C for about
30–35 min. Validate crumb density and moisture; do not overproof.
3. Danisa-style butter biscuits
Formula. 10–18 kg low-protein flour; butter or margarine 50–60%; powdered sugar 35–40%; eggs 10–15%; optional milk powder
and vanilla.
Process and control. Cream fat and sugar, add eggs slowly, fold flour briefly, pipe or stamp, and bake near 170°C without steam.
Control spread, browning, and moisture. Shelf life requires validation.
4. French croissant
Formula. Flour 100%; liquid 55%; sugar 10–12%; dough butter 4–5%; salt 2%; yeast 1.5%; roll-in butter 30–35% of dough mass.
Process and control. Mix lightly, chill, laminate with controlled folds, sheet to about 4 mm, cut and shape, proof below 26°C, bake
near 190°C. Heavy steam is not a default; validate the oven response.
5. Pain au chocolat
Formula. Use croissant dough with two chocolate batons per piece.
Process and control. Cut rectangles, place batons, roll seam-side down, proof cool, and bake near 190°C. Protect layers and
standardize chocolate weight.
6. Pullman sandwich bread
Formula. 18 kg flour; water 58–60%; sugar 5–6%; shortening or margarine 5%; milk powder 3%; salt 2%; yeast 0.8%.
Process and control. Mix to full development, mold into cylinders, place in lidded pans, proof below rim, close lids, and bake near
200°C for 35–40 min. Slice only after complete cooling.
7. Soft burger buns
Formula. 18 kg flour; liquid 55–60%; sugar 8–10%; fat 7–10%; salt about 1.8–2%; yeast 1%; optional improver.
Process and control. Mix to windowpane, scale 80–90 g, round, egg-wash and seed, proof near 28–30°C with humidity, and bake
near 210°C for 11–13 min. Supply by fixed case count and weight.
8. Pain beurre
Formula. 18 kg flour; water 52%; butter or margarine 10–12%; sugar 7–8%; salt 2%; yeast around 0.9–1%.
Process and control. Add fat after dough forms, develop until silky, scale 200–400 g, mold, proof, score, and bake near
195–200°C. Control fat absorption and aroma.
9. Pound cake
Formula. Equal flour, sugar, and fat by weight; eggs approximately 90–100% of flour; baking powder 1.5%; flavor.
Process and control. Cream fat and sugar, add eggs gradually, fold sifted flour and leavener, pan, and bake near 160–165°C
without steam for 45–50 min. Cool completely before slicing.
10. Hard dough bread
Formula. 18 kg flour; water 48–52%; sugar 0–7%; fat 0–6%; salt 2%; yeast 0.4–0.6%.
Process and control. Mix stiff dough carefully, mold enough to create a tight crumb, pan, proof longer, and bake near 195–230°C.
Do not damage the mixer by forcing an unvalidated load.

11. Pain aux raisins
Formula. Croissant dough plus cold pastry cream and approximately 2–2.5 kg soaked raisins per 18 kg flour base.
Process and control. Sheet cold dough, spread cream, add raisins, roll, slice 2 cm wheels, proof cool, egg-wash, and bake near
190–195°C. Keep filling cold and control leakage.
12. Pain au lait
Formula. 18 kg flour; milk 55–58%; sugar 12%; butter 8%; salt 1.8%; yeast 1.0–1.2%.
Process and control. Mix smooth dough, scale 60 g, shape finger rolls, decorate, proof, egg-wash, and bake near 200–210°C for
10–12 min.
13. Brioche or brioche Vendenne
Formula. Flour 100%; eggs 30–50%; sugar 15–18%; butter 25–40%; salt 2%; yeast 2%; cold liquid as required.
Process and control. Develop dough before adding butter gradually, chill to firm, shape or braid, proof cool, egg-wash, and bake
near 170–180°C. Protect butter from melting.
14. Long sandwich buns
Formula. 18 kg flour; liquid 60%; sugar 4%; shortening 4%; salt 2%; yeast 0.8%; optional malt.
Process and control. Mold to approximately 25 cm, proof near 28°C, score, apply light steam, and bake near 215°C for about 14
min.
15. Coconut crunch bread
Formula. 18 kg flour; water 52%; sugar 12–15%; fat 8%; coconut 10%; salt 1.5–2%; yeast 1%.
Process and control. Add coconut late in mixing, shape, top with coconut and sugar, proof, and bake near 185°C for 25 min.
Monitor topping burn.
16. Whole-grain health loaf
Formula. 50–66% whole-wheat flour; remainder white flour; water 65–68%; honey or molasses 5%; oil 4%; salt 2%; yeast about
0.7%.
Process and control. Autolyse 20 min, mix briefly, bulk ferment, shape into 500 g loaves, top with seeds, proof, and bake near
200°C. Use truthful ingredient and nutrition labeling.
17. Garlic, herb and cheese flatbread
Formula. 18 kg flour; water 62%; oil 6%; salt 2%; dried garlic and herbs; cheese topping.
Process and control. Mix high-hydration dough, ferment, stretch into flat shapes, cut decorative openings, oil, top, and bake hot
near 230°C. Separate cheese and raw-meat handling.
18. Sausage roll
Formula. Short or puff pastry; filling based on seasoned meat; source examples range from 6–15 kg filling per 10–18 kg flour.
Process and control. Keep pastry cold, pipe uniform filling, seal, score, egg-wash, and bake near 200°C for approximately 22 min.
Validate meat cooking and cold holding.
19. Fudgy brownie
