1 Executive recommendation
Build the opening business around six dependable core products: family bread, condensed milk buns, sugar
doughnuts, puff puff, fish rolls and beef meat pies. Add chicken shawarma only when its service station and
raw-meat workflow pass a live trial. Start the frozen counter with one approved soft-serve base and small milk
pops; add the larger pops only after customers accept the price and freezer throughput is measured.
This is a commercial hypothesis, not a forecast of Nkambe demand. No current Nkambe customer survey,
supplier quotations, competitor-weight audit, footfall count or sales history was supplied. The menu prices

The new 30 kWh battery figure is recorded as nominal energy at each site. Solar array capacity remains

electrical rating alone is not its full energy cost.
Batch and tray capacity
A 40 × 60 cm tray has 0.24 m² of surface; sixteen trays have 3.84 m² before spacing and usable-area losses. That
does not specify how many tins fit, the permissible rack weight, dough-mixer capacity or hourly output. Draw a
tray layout with the actual tins. A trial of 12 milk buns per tray would mean 192 buns per rack, but it is an
illustration, not a rated capacity. Do not fill all sixteen trays until airflow and bake uniformity have been checked.
The recipe library gives pilot quantities and a x5 planning production quantity. No mixer bowl or minimum
machine charge has been verified. Use a smaller mixer or hand pilot if the spiral cannot mix the pilot load safely.
Production quantities are for purchasing and scale-up trials, not permission to overload equipment. The
professional mixing pot is not assumed to be a validated pasteurizer.
Weights and formula conventions
All formulation quantities are grams, including liquids, to avoid density errors. Water alone may be
approximated as 1 g/ml. Milk, oil, condensed milk and fruit puree must be weighed. An egg is provisionally 50 g
edible contents; use the weighed mass, not a fixed count. Vegetable prices are per peeled/trimmed edible
kilogram; meat is boneless purchase weight; canned fish is drained weight. Convert supplier gross-pack prices
before use.
Yields are trial estimates after stated losses. Cost cooked components by saleable output. Frying oil entries
cover usage/disposal, not the initial fryer fill; budget the initial fill separately as working stock.

4 Core recipes and production standards
Use each assembly card together with its named base formulas. A base amount on a card is usable prepared
mass, not necessarily a whole base batch. Scaling factor = required usable mass / base usable yield. The
ingredient table then scales every raw ingredient by that factor. This prevents the common error of giving a
filling batch that cannot fill the stated number of pieces.
All oven temperatures and process times below are starting trials for the diesel rotary oven, not manufacturer
settings or recipe validation. Run a small load first, weigh cooled output and correct the process sheet. Use
fresh instant dry yeast; confirm flour consistency and water temperature each day.
S1 Food handling controls used throughout
Use a cleaned, calibrated probe. For this plan, adopt a single conservative house target of at least 75°C at the
thickest centre for 30 seconds when cooking meat, poultry, fish and their fillings, and when reheating cooked
portions. Check actual food temperature, not the oven dial. This updates the earlier separate meat limits for
simpler staff operation; frozen dairy mix processing retains its separate F0 instructions. These are external
benchmarks and a house choice, not verified Cameroon legal limits. UK food business cooking guidance
Keep chilled perishable ingredients at or below 5°C. For cooked food requiring cooling, use shallow pans,
smaller portions or an ice bath: cool from 57°C to 21°C within 2 hours and to 5°C within 6 hours total. Log start,
intermediate and final temperatures; a deep pot in a refrigerator does not prove compliance. These thresholds
follow the cited FDA model guidance. FDA Food Code 2022 sections 3-501.14–16
6 / 83

Adopt a house hot-holding minimum of 63°C, checked at the food rather than the cabinet dial. This follows UK
business guidance and is a separate house benchmark; it is not a statement of Cameroon law. Food with an
unknown time/temperature history is not rescued by reheating. UK food business cooking guidance
House pilot policy: refrigerate components promptly, make only same-day quantities, and discard unsold high-
risk prepared food at close unless a separately documented, validated storage plan applies. The same-day
policy does not make a temperature-abused product safe. Quick pickles and cooked sauces remain refrigerated;
their acidity has not been validated for ambient storage.
Condensed milk formulation
Sweetened condensed milk supplies added sugar, milk solids and water. It is different from unsweetened
evaporated milk and dry milk powder. For development calculations only, assume 45% added sucrose, 27%
water and 28% milk solids including about 8% fat; these fractions sum to 100%. Naturally occurring lactose is
inside the milk-solids fraction, so do not add total label sugars a second time.
Check the purchased label. One manufacturer example reports 55.9 g total sugars and 8.1 g fat per 100 g; total
sugars are not the same as added sucrose. No brand specification is assumed to apply to your local tin. Nestlé
example product specification
In B02, 150 g condensed milk adds approximately 67.5 g sucrose, 40.5 g water and 42 g milk solids, in addition
to the separately listed 80 g sugar and 430 g water. The trial substitution on P02 approximates those functions,
not identical flavour. For cost: 150 g condensed milk at 3,000 FCFA/kg costs 450 FCFA; 68 g sugar + 42 g whole
milk powder + 40 g water costs about 244 FCFA, saving roughly 206 FCFA per B02 pilot, before any quality/yield
change. Test both blind.
Use condensed milk selectively in buns, banana cake, soaked cake and frozen dairy products. Keep P01 bread,
plain puff puff and most savouries free of it in the baseline to protect entry-level cost. Added dairy glaze
changes the holding requirements even if the underlying bread is normally sold at ambient temperature.

1. Weigh ingredients. Reserve 30 g of the listed water; add only if the flour absorbs it. The stated yield
assumes all 600 g is used.
2. Mix flour, water, sugar and yeast about 3 minutes at low speed. Add salt, then mix 4–7 minutes at the
mixer’s dough-development speed. Add margarine during the final 2 minutes. Stop at a thin elastic
window and a dough temperature near 26–28°C.
3. Rest covered 15 minutes. Divide, pre-round, rest 10 minutes, then mould and tin as specified on the
product card.
4. Trial proof at 30–35°C with enough humidity to prevent skinning, approximately 45–75 minutes. Bake
when a gentle indentation returns slowly; do not use the clock alone.

One controlled route from production to the customer
1. Receive sound ingredients and keep raw proteins chilled under S1. Thaw covered in the refrigerator,
below ready-to-eat food. Weigh raw portions with dedicated utensils; wash hands and clean/sanitize the
area before handling cooked food. Marinade is not a preservation treatment. Discard raw-contact
marinade after use; never use it as a finishing sauce.
2. Fully cook using the appropriate method below. Use the S1 house cooking target throughout; check the
thickest portions at the oven's slowest-heating positions. Record batch identity, time and measured food
temperature. Browning is a quality cue, not evidence of core doneness. Never partly cook meat or
chicken, refrigerate it, then finish later. USDA guidance on partial cooking
3. Start active cooling immediately after cooking. Arrange patties or fillets in a single layer; spread
strips/fillings in shallow food-safe pans with circulation around them. Use a blast chiller if available, or
another method demonstrated to meet S1. A large refrigerator/freezer does not establish sufficient
cooling capacity. Protect loosely covered food during cooling, then cover fully when chilled. Log the
cooling checkpoints; do not leave trays on the counter to reach room temperature. FDA employee
cooling guidance
4. Weigh chilled usable output with clean utensils, quickly enough to maintain temperature control. Portion
into shallow labelled containers; do not pack hot food into stacked deep takeaway boxes. Label recipe,
species, batch, cook time, cooling completion, weight, use-by and reheat count. Patties may be formed
before cooking; weigh after chilling to check shrinkage. Discard bones, gristle and unsuitable pieces
before calculating saleable yield.
5. Transport covered portions with sufficient ice packs in clean insulated carriers, or suitable refrigerated
transport. Record dispatch and arrival food temperatures; maintain the S1 cold limit during the journey
and sales-site storage. Keep raw and cooked foods separate. Do not rely on journey length alone.
Quarantine and discard food with an unknown unsafe temperature history; reheating cannot repair every

for the complete portion to reach the target; do not add cold juices afterwards.

F0 Frozen dairy processing and machine control
Gravity machines need a smooth mix that flows reliably. Use the actual manufacturer’s approved range, hopper

For on-site dairy-mix processing, a published technical benchmark is to heat every part of the mix to at least
69°C for 30 minutes or 80°C for 25 seconds. This report uses the 80°C/25-second route only as a development
reference for an agitated, measured and professionally validated process, not a claim that heating an ordinary

After a validated heat step, chill promptly under S1 and target 1–4°C before aging/loading. Never put warm mix

density is 1.09/1.30 = 0.838 kg/L. Measure rather than assuming either machine reaches that value. University of

How the model works
1. Batch ingredient cost = sum of ingredient grams × FCFA/kg ÷ 1,000. For a prepared component,
recursively include all its raw ingredients and divide by its usable output, not raw input mass.
2. Ingredient cost per saleable unit = costed batch / stated saleable units. Explicit pastry/dough QC
allowances are charged to the product. Full unused filling batches are not automatically charged twice:
each assembly card charges only its required usable share, so unused safe stock must be recorded and
eventually sold or written off.
3. Variable cost per unit = ingredient cost + packaging + other variable allowance. Contribution = selling
price − variable cost. Contribution margin = contribution / selling price. Contribution does not pay the
owner until overhead and other obligations are met.
4. Other-variable allowances are placeholders for production energy/diesel, incremental cleaning/water and
variable handling/delivery. Provisionally allocate 60% to energy/diesel, 20% to incremental
cleaning/water and 20% to variable handling/delivery, then replace with actual logs. Paid shift wages and
base route transport are in overhead below; do not charge the same expense in both places.
5. Sensitivity at 20% ingredient inflation: contribution falls by 0.20 × ingredient cost per unit. At 80% sell-
through, assume all planned variable cost was incurred, including packaging: effective contribution per
sold unit = price − full planned variable cost / 0.80. This conservatively charges unsold units to those
sold.
6. All prices and calculations are gross cash planning values without a tax determination. If an applicable tax
must be remitted from the displayed selling price, remove it from revenue before computing
contribution. Obtain the business-specific treatment locally; no Cameroon tax status is assumed.

Demand and replenishment method
Start with customer commitments and small trials. During the first three days, prepare at most one economical
equipment-minimum batch per launch product, split between opening and later waves where fermentation
allows. Record sales in hourly blocks. Days 4–14: forecast from comparable trading days plus paid orders;
prepare about 60% of the forecast first, then release a second batch only when remaining sales and production
lead time justify it. These percentages are management trial rules, not demand evidence.
Use a practical reorder trigger: expected sales during production plus delivery lead time, plus a small measured
buffer, minus saleable stock already present. Do not replenish perishable stock close to closing simply to make
a counter look full. Record unsold stock by cause: forecast error, poor quality, temperature failure, transport
damage, portion rejects or customer preference.

Battery runtime illustration
At each site, assume 30 kWh nominal × 80% usable fraction × 90% conversion efficiency = 21.6 kWh delivered
AC energy. These 80% and 90% figures are assumptions, not the battery specification. Without solar/grid input,
that supports a constant 1 kW load for about 21.6 hours, 2 kW for 10.8 hours, 5 kW for 4.3 hours, or 10 kW for
2.2 hours, subject to discharge power limits. Actual cycling refrigerator loads should be measured over a full
day.
A twelve-hour overnight critical load averaging 1 kW needs 12 kWh delivered AC, equivalent to about 16.7 kWh
nominal under the same assumptions. Set the evening reserve using measured essential refrigeration plus
margin and forecast morning demand. Do not consume the cold-chain reserve on optional fryers or a second
frozen-dessert machine.
PV energy cannot be estimated from the inverter size. Use measured site generation or actual installed panel
kWp × site-specific equivalent sun hours × system performance ratio. The illustrative daily electrical totals
above are about 45.6 kWh at production and 62.6 kWh at sales before omitted loads. Neither is covered merely
because a 30 kWh battery exists; daily replenishment and evening reserve matter.

Allergens and cleaning
Create a matrix from actual supplier labels. Wheat is present in breads, pastry, doughnuts, wrappers, buns, cake
and cookies; milk in enriched doughs, creamy fillings, cheese, many sauces and frozen dairy; egg in egg wash,
cakes, egg rolls and many commercial mayonnaise products; fish in P06; sesame in B35/tahini and optional bun
topping; peanuts in F02 and F08; mustard in B28. Margarine, sausage meat, coatings, chocolate and stabilizer
blends can introduce soy, milk or other allergens. Supplier labels override this preliminary list.

Use dedicated fish frying equipment or a documented separation plan; shared oil can transfer allergens and
flavours to doughnuts and fries. Never label a product allergen-free merely because its formula omits the
allergen. Separate raw meat tools, cooked-food tools, bakery utensils and frozen-dessert tools. Clean and
sanitize according to the chemical label; do not mix chemicals or invent sanitizer concentrations.
Power interruption and cold-chain response
1. Record outage start, cold cabinet temperatures and battery state immediately. Keep doors shut and
reserve power for food-critical refrigeration.
2. Pause new high-risk preparation if safe cooling or storage cannot be maintained. Use temperature-
verified backup storage/transport if available.
3. Record product temperatures and duration, not just the freezer display. Quarantine food with an
unknown history; do not assume refreezing restores safety.
4. Restart machines only after safe equipment checks and the manufacturer’s sanitation/recovery procedure.
Discard questionable hopper mix and record the loss.
5. Review the event, reserve setting and load schedule before resuming the full menu.
