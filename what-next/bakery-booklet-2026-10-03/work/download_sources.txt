the final crispy crust right in front of the buyer.

1. Premium Authentic French Baguette (Par-Bake Baseline)
Industrial Formulation (18kg Base): Flour: 18.00 kg (100%) | Chilled Water: 10.38 kg (57.6%) | Crushed Ice:
1.50 kg (8.4%) | Salt: 360 g (2.0%) | Instant Yeast: 90 g (0.5%)
Mixing & Mechanical Processing: Target final dough temperature 23°C. Mix 3m Speed 1, 10m autolyse, 5m
Speed 2. Bulk ferment 45m. Divide at 300g, rest 15m. Run through molder with medium roller gap. Proof for
60m at 26°C, 75% humidity.

incisions. Inject heavy steam for 15 seconds. For Par-Baking (logistics advantage): Bake for 15 mins at 220°C

at 240°C for explosive, crackling freshness.

Mixing & Mechanical Processing: Mix 5m Speed 1, 6m Speed 2. Dough must be dense and uniform. Bulk
ferment 30m. Divide into heavy 600g blocks. Pass through molder with tight rollers to degas completely and

Baking, Dampening & Mechanical Controls: Bake at 190°C (374°F) for 35 minutes in the rotary oven. Keep
the damper completely closed for the first 25 minutes to trap internal moisture, ensuring a soft, shreddable

Commercial Baguette Production Guide
Optimizing artisan formulation for 50kg spiral mixers, mechanical molders, and 16-tray rotary ovens.
Part 1: The Challenge of the Baguette
Authentic French baguettes are notoriously difficult to master because their simplicity leaves no room for
error. Relying entirely on flour, water, salt, and yeast, the final quality depends entirely on technique and
environmental control.
 High Hydration vs. High Tension: Dough typically requires 65% to 75% hydration to achieve an open
 crumb (l'alvéolage). The challenge lies in maintaining structural tension in a wet dough without overhandling.
 The Art of Shaping: Bakers must build surface tension while avoiding over-degassing, which destroys
 internal cell structure.
 Precise Scoring: Slashing with a razor blade at a 30-to-45-degree angle ensures an even rise (oven spring)
 instead of side ruptures.
 The Steam Dilemma: Steam is critical during the first 10 minutes of baking to keep the skin elastic and
 gelatinize surface starches for a crackly crust.
Part 2: Adapting to Industrial Equipment
Transitioning from hand-shaping to machinery requires structural adjustments to your process to ensure
consistent quality at scale.
 Spiral Mixer Friction: Commercial mixers generate intense heat. Ice or chilled water must be utilized to
 maintain a strict final dough temperature of 23°C to 24°C.
 Molder Tolerance: To prevent sticking or tearing in mechanical rollers, target a highly stable 65% to 68%
 hydration level. Avoid overly tight rollers to protect internal air bubbles.
 Rotary Convection Management: Moving hot air dries dough rapidly. Heavy steam must be injected
 immediately upon loading before the crust bakes shut.

2. The Strategy for Back-to-Back Batches
In a commercial setup with a single 16-tray rotary oven, your production bottleneck is the 25-minute oven cycle (22
mins bake + 3 mins recovery/reheat). If you mix batches sequentially without a delay, Batch 2 will sit in the proofer 25
minutes too long, leading to over-proofing, structural collapse, and pale, flat baguettes.
The Master Rule: Your mixing cycle must match your baking cycle. You must introduce a strict 45-minute stagger
interval between starting each batch in the spiral mixer. This exact cadence synchronizes the mixer, divider, molder,
proofer, and oven perfectly.

4. Critical Industrial Controls
 The Proofer Bottleneck: Ensure your proofer can hold both Rack 1 and Rack 2 simultaneously, as their proofing
windows overlap by 15 minutes (from hour 01:48 to 02:15).
 Oven Thermal Recovery: A 16-tray rotary oven loses significant heat when the door is opened. Always preheat to
240°C so it drops to your ideal 220°C target when the rack is locked in. The 23 minutes between bakes is mandatory for
the diesel burner to recover its thermal mass.
 Ice Water Contingency: For Batch 2, your water must be just as cold (4°C) as Batch 1. If your water chiller cannot
supply ice water continuously, use heavy flaked ice crushed directly into the mix to keep the final dough temperature
locked at 23-24°C.
