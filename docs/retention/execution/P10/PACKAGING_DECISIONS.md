## Willingness-to-pay research protocol — prepared, not run

1. Product/commercial owner first approves recruitment, data handling, script, compensation (if any) and contact authority. Use a proposed small discovery sample of **6–8 businesses** covering single-site retailers, 2–5-site operators and their finance/accountant decision partners; this is a planning target, not a recruited cohort or statistical market estimate. Include participants willing to reject the product; avoid only friendly/high-engagement users.
2. Document role, purchase authority, country/sector, sites/users, current workflow/tools, actual recent failure/rework examples and cost-to-serve needs. Ask permission before recording; retain only necessary research data under an approved schedule. Never ask for production credentials or unredacted financial records for pricing discovery.
3. Show the same honest scope card above, current exclusions, a synthetic walkthrough and proposed support/data-exit terms. Distinguish available, tested locally and pending capability. Do not anchor with fabricated competitor prices or unsupported compliance/offline claims. No external comparisons are needed for this packet.
4. Ask neutrally: Which weekly job would improve? What would still prevent adoption? Who approves purchase? Which current spend/time would change? What budget range, billing interval and onboarding/support arrangement would be acceptable for this exact scope? What would make it unacceptable? Record currency/period/conditions and refusals separately from enthusiasm. Willingness statements are not purchases.
5. Once authorized evidence and cost-to-serve exist, commercial owner may formulate explicit candidate quotes for a later controlled offer test. Confirm they cover the same included users/sites/support/archive rights; do not secretly vary exit rights or make cancellation harder to increase conversion. Track quote version, channel, decision-maker, acceptance/rejection/reason and actual paid outcome separately. P10 neither sets nor sends a quote.
6. Analyze by business segment and job, using ranges and counterexamples; separate implementation gaps, trust/support objections and affordability. Small-sample results are hypotheses, not validated price elasticity. Revisit inclusions when support/data-migration cost contradicts the package. No success percentage or commercial launch gate is invented here.

Proposed measurement handoff: activation time from onboarding start and data-ready state separately; active workflow days per eligible trading week; module/team usage by verified domain outcome rather than navigation clicks; offer-to-accepted-to-paid conversion with explicit denominators; support time per business; cancellation receipt/completion and optional reasons with missing reasons reported as unknown. Reuse P02 definitions where compatible and have data owner approve new definitions. No telemetry or cohort data was read/changed in P10.
