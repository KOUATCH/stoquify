01 / Owner overview

1 — Context once, clearly

Keep organization, location, period, currency, and checked time together. Every drill-through carries valid scope.

2 — One issue, one next step

Deduplicate repeated cards using source identity. Show who acts next. Keep approval and completion distinct.

3 — Evidence on demand

Expose business meaning first. Keep full lineage and technical diagnostics available to authorized reviewers.

4 — Setup adapts to maturity

Completed setup no longer dominates the daily landing. Critical setup blockers remain visible.

Build from the existing brief, KPI, action queue, and proof primitives. See F01–03, F05, F07–10. Measure time to identify and open the right issue.

02 / Cashier workspace

1 — A compact readiness strip

Collapse register configuration when ready. Expand the real blocking state only when necessary. Remove stacked fixed-height offsets.

2 — Space for the catalog

Prioritize scanning/search, readable units, and stable item controls. Reuse existing touch/keyboard support.

3 — A stable cart and total

Cart identity, totals, and next valid action remain clear. On small screens, use an explicit catalog/cart switch rather than squeezing both columns.

4 — Recovery preserves truth

Uncertain is not failed or complete. Keep sale identity, replay keys, leases, and revision controls unchanged.

Desktop composition is illustrated here. The narrow preview shows reading order, not a validated POS interaction. See F06 and F15; test actual keyboard and touch checkout in isolated fixtures.

03 / Accountant handoff

1 — The case is the center

Keep the client mandate, period, and source record visible. This extends the existing accountant work desk.

2 — Evidence before decision

Make review sequence clear while retaining fresh identity checks, notes, revision checks, and maker-checker rules.

3 — Who acts next?

Reduce chasing by showing the responsible role, actual delivery state, and remaining prerequisite.

4 — Preserve the work queue

Review one case and return to the same context. Never imply that submitted evidence is accepted.
