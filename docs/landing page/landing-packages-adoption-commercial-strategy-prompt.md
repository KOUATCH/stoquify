# Landing Packages and Adoption Strategy Prompt

## Refined Prompt

Act as a multidisciplinary principal product-commercialization team for Stoquify, combining SaaS product strategy, enterprise architecture, finance, billing operations, OHADA-domain operations, UX writing, and go-to-market design.

From a thorough inspection of the live repository, redesign the landing page's current Adoption Plan so that the offers genuinely reflect what Stoquify can deliver, create coherent combinations for SMB customers, preserve strong customer value, and leave the business room to monetize sustainably.

Your work must:

1. Audit the canonical module catalog, dependency rules, entitlement behavior, package and subscription readiness, billing/provider boundaries, release status, operational cost drivers, and the current landing-page presentation. Treat repository evidence as truth and distinguish implemented, controlled-release, beta, proposed, and missing capabilities.
2. Identify practical customer segments and buying situations for OHADA-region SMBs, including retail, distribution, multi-branch operations, finance and close control, and people/payroll formalization.
3. Propose a simple but sophisticated package architecture. Separate the always-present platform foundation, primary adoption paths, dependency-aware add-ons, implementation services, support, country packs, and provider integrations. Do not sell internal modules or allow reports and analytics to bypass source-module access.
4. Recommend sustainable pricing hypotheses and charging units, clearly labeled as hypotheses rather than approved prices. Consider organization, location/terminal, active employee, provider connection, country pack, implementation scope, and support level. Protect essential security, tenant isolation, auditability, backup, and baseline evidence from punitive feature gating.
5. Compare the approach with current official packaging patterns from relevant ERP, POS, and payroll products, using primary sources only. Use this as a market sanity check, not as permission to copy foreign pricing into OHADA markets.
6. Redesign and implement the bilingual English/French landing section as a modern, enterprise-grade Packages and Adoption Paths experience. Make dependencies, target use cases, commercial drivers, controlled availability, and rollout expectations understandable without presenting fictional checkout, instant provisioning, unsupported prices, or guaranteed regulatory outcomes.
7. Preserve the existing landing design language, accessibility, responsive behavior, localization conventions, and focused code boundaries. Use accessible controls for option sets, familiar icons, restrained cards, and concise, accurate copy.
8. Add focused content and browser gates. Verify both locales, desktop and mobile behavior, keyboard-accessible views, absence of horizontal overflow and page errors, and saved screenshots/evidence under `what-next/`.
9. Produce a concise final report that states what changed, what was verified, the recommended commercial model, the control-plane dependencies that remain before self-service monetization, and residual launch risk.

Do not implement package database tables, payment-provider billing, entitlement changes, broad module enforcement, destructive migrations, deployment, or unsupported commercial commitments as part of this landing-page task.
