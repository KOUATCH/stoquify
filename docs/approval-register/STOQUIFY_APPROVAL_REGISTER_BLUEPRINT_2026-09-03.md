# Blueprint: Stoquify Approval Register

Prepared 3 September 2026 · Version 1 · **PROPOSED — NOT IMPLEMENTED OR ADOPTED**

This design creates no approval, appoints no person, and changes no X1 gate. Product labels and routes below are proposed. The operating guide explains the screens and information the owner will supply.

## What we are building

A reusable **Governance → Approvals** module in Stoquify. It records who may decide, presents the exact material for a decision, authenticates the person confirming it, and retains a verifiable receipt with a stable protected link. X1 is the first integration. Other governance and release decisions can use the same register; purchasing, payroll, and accounting retain their domain workflows and controls.

Everyday flow: **prepare request → review exact version → confirm identity and decision → receive references → check eligibility at the next gate**. The person supplies the decision and business context. Stoquify generates record IDs, native decision timestamps, document hashes, verification-event references, and source-record links.

## Language locked

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Authority appointment | A recorded grant from someone entitled to appoint the person, covering a decision type, organization, environment, limits, and validity period. An application role alone is insufficient. |
| Approval request | The versioned subject submitted for review. A draft can have a request ID without being approved. |
| Decision receipt | Evidence of an actual approve, reject, or request-revision action, bound to the person, authority, exact revision, policy, and verification event. |
| SOURCE RECORD URI | The permanent, access-controlled page for that record and its verification evidence. Generated by the register; separate from expiring storage download URLs. |
| Gate eligibility | Whether approval and all other required conditions are valid for the action being attempted now. Approval alone does not establish a technical pass. |

## First-time establishment: no pre-existing register required

1. **Create a setup draft.** A deployment administrator enables only the setup surface. Stoquify creates a provisional setup record and upload references. No earlier approval ID is requested. All resulting records are visibly pending.
2. **Identify the actual owner and authority basis.** Supply real name, organization/project, account, and the source establishing authority to adopt this process. For an organization, this might be a governance mandate or executive appointment. For an individually owned project, an owner declaration can initiate setup, supported by existing project ownership records and identity checks. It is not automatically accepted as authority over a separate organization or other tenants.
3. **Verify establishment evidence.** A designated setup verifier checks identity-to-account binding and authority against the source, using an independently established contact or ownership record. Store who checked what, when, and the outcome. An administrator account or email address alone does not establish authority. Missing verifier or source leaves `SETUP_PENDING_VERIFICATION`; module development and draft preparation can continue.
4. **Adopt the process and appoint people.** The verified owner confirms the exact charter version, in-app signing method, scopes, role conflicts, validity limits, revocation responsibilities and retention policy. Initial appointments are bound to that record; each appointee accepts with their own verified account. Compatible scopes can be combined where policy permits. Preserve independent release review and stricter domain separation rules.
5. **Activate after setup checks.** A bounded installation command, restricted to the deployment operator, checks the completed establishment receipt, storage access controls, required roles and policy version, and records activation atomically. It cannot create missing approvals. Ordinary approval actions cannot appoint their own root authority.

The trust boundary begins with a real owner and an explicit establishment act. The system stores and verifies that act without demanding an infinite chain of earlier register IDs. If the owner is also the administrator, record both roles transparently and retain separate verification. An agent may prepare the charter and perform checks, but must not impersonate owner or verifier.

## Proposed user experience

| Screen | What the person does | What Stoquify handles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Supplies owner/authority evidence, appoints people, adopts the process. | Provisional IDs, private upload references, completeness checks, activation history. |
| My approvals | Opens an assigned request; reviews exact version and scope. | Assignment, organization filtering, missing information, expiry and conflicts. |
| Decision | Selects Approve, Reject, or Request revision; records reasons/conditions; confirms identity. | Fresh-auth challenge bound to this revision, authority checks and server time. |
| Record | Reads decision, approved material, signer, authority and history. | Immutable IDs, verification receipt, hashes, stable link and Copy X1 fields. |
| Authorities | Owner appoints, renews or revokes a scoped authority. | Prevents grants outside issuer's scope; retains changes and appointee acceptance. |
| Gate readiness | Reviews remaining X1 conditions and next permitted action. | Resolves approval validity and technical evidence without inventing a pass. |

Proposed protected route: `/{locale}/dashboard/governance/approvals/{recordId}`. The real HTTPS origin comes from approved deployment configuration; never display invented URLs as live records. Follow existing locale, theme and navigation conventions. Status messages explain missing information and who can supply it. Approval requires connectivity; stale/offline screens cannot confirm decisions.

## The spine

### State and data model

Primary state lives in PostgreSQL through Prisma. PDFs are exports; checked-in Markdown, JSON reports and screenshots are not the authority database. Binary evidence lives in private storage with immutable object versions. IDs are server-generated random identifiers, unique within their record type; human labels may be displayed separately.

| Proposed entity | Essential fields and invariants |
| --- | --- |
| GovernanceRegister / PolicyRevision | Owning organization; installation/environment scope; establishment evidence; adopted policy revision; status/version; activation and revocation events. Draft policy never active by default. |
| AuthorityAppointment | Organization; real subject ID; issuing authority; permitted decision types/environments/limits; effective/expiry times; source revision; acceptance receipt. Issuer cannot grant broader or longer authority than they possess. |
| ApprovalRequest / RequestRevision | Organization; type; subject reference; exact scope; policy revision; requester; expected version; required authority scopes; submission time; document-set digest. Submitted revisions cannot be edited. |
| ApprovalArtifact | Organization; revision; private storage key and immutable object version; SHA-256; MIME type; size; availability and verification state. Compute hashes from stored bytes, not a supplied digest. |
| ApprovalDecision | Organization; revision; actual actor; authority appointment version; decision/reason/conditions; native server decision time; expiry; idempotency key and payload hash. Append-only; correction creates another record. |
| ApprovalVerification | Decision or establishment reference; method; exact confirmed payload digest; authentication assurance; verifier when applicable; verifiedAt; evidence references; outcome. No passwords or reusable challenges retained. |
| ApprovalEvent | Organization; aggregate/version; type; actor; server time; before/after references; reason; correlation ID. Mandatory events commit with the decision; approval history is not cascade-deleted. |

Use tenant-scoped composite foreign keys where supported, uniqueness constraints, and mandatory organization predicates. Reject cross-organization references even for valid guessed IDs. Preserve historical identity snapshots after account departure with restricted access; invalidate future use where policy requires. Organization deletion follows an explicit retention/export policy, not cascade deletion of the register.

Store development governance in the actual owner's organization. A fixture tenant is an approval target, not proof that a seeded user is the owner or signer. Keep the real human signer and test persona separate; any approved evidence identity mapping must be explicit. Never invent fixture memberships to make an intake pass.

Request progress: `DRAFT → PENDING → APPROVED | REJECTED | REVISION_REQUESTED`. A new revision requires new submission. Separately compute effective validity: `UNVERIFIED | VALID | EXPIRED | REVOKED | SUPERSEDED | INVALID_SCOPE`. Preserve the original decision after it becomes ineligible. Conditions must be fulfilled before eligibility; an agent must not interpret free text as a blanket waiver.

### Contract and trust boundary

Proposed boundary: `actions/governance/approval-register.actions.ts` over `services/governance/approval-register.service.ts`. Mutations use strict schemas, `protect`, registered permissions, and server-derived organization/actor. Proposed permissions cover read, prepare, submit, scoped approve, verify imported signature, manage authorities, export and revoke. A broad UI role label does not grant these powers.

Commands: `createDraft`, `attachArtifact`, `submitRevision`, `prepareDecisionChallenge`, `recordDecision`, `verifyImportedSignature`, `revokeDecision`, `appointAuthority`, `activateRegister`. Inputs include request/revision ID, expected version, decision, reason and idempotency key. Server supplies actor ID, organization, timestamps, IDs, authority resolution and computed digests. Historical paper imports use separately named `claimedSignedAt` with supporting evidence; never backdate native events.

`recordDecision` binds a short-lived, single-use challenge to actor, session, request revision, document-set digest, exact decision text, conditions, expiry and policy revision. Use current password step-up and fresh session (proposed maximum age five minutes). Display what is being confirmed before authentication. Recheck authority and document revision inside the committing transaction.

Native output is an **authenticated internal decision receipt**. Its verification reference identifies the actual confirmation event. It is not a third-party certificate or a cryptographic signature made with a person's private key. The owner adopts this method during establishment. An organization requiring another method may require a verified provider event instead.

Verification returns current status, decision ID, revision, signer, authority reference, method, verified decision time, expiry, revocation/supersession and scope digests. A typed ID, PDF stamp, matching hash or arbitrary URL must never suffice for `VALID`.

### Sync and failure handling

Upload first into quarantined private storage. Validate type, size, malware policy and object ownership before submission. Approval is a short transaction writing decision, mandatory audit event, consumed challenge and version update together. Use serializable transactions or explicit version locks consistent with release-control patterns. PDF generation/indexing run afterward through a retriable job/outbox. Export failure leaves the committed decision visible with `EXPORT_PENDING`; retry cannot create a second decision.

Scope idempotency keys to organization/actor/operation. Same key and payload returns the committed result; different payload returns conflict. Serialize concurrent approve/revoke and revise/approve. Before downstream action, check live validity. Where action and register share a database, perform eligibility check and protected state change within one controlled transaction. External evidence jobs bind their run to approval revisions and recheck before accepting results or release.

Unavailable verification yields a named pending/blocked condition. History remains readable where safe. Never infer approval from a timeout. Revocation stops future consumption; it does not silently undo completed business transactions.

## Decisions and tradeoffs

| Decision | Default and tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Signing method | Native authenticated confirmation first; no commercial provider required. Requires explicit organizational adoption and secure operation. External signatures remain optional. |
| Reuse boundary | Reuse auth/RBAC/release-service patterns; add general records. Domain approval tables still own business transactions, preventing a generic button from bypassing their invariants. |
| Scope | X1 product, security/data, authority appointments and independent gate review first. Policy/change decisions follow. Financial execution is outside phase one. |
| Human roles | Default separate product/security decision makers and a reviewer independent of implementation/capture. Adopted policy records allowed overlaps; preserve stricter existing rules. No arbitrary five-person requirement. |
| Validity | Explicit expiry. Existing X1 product form proposes 90 days; security/capture covers the actual run window. No silent perpetual approvals. |
| Evidence custody | Dedicated private storage and protected links. Existing inventory image uploads are unsuitable. Actual storage provider remains an infrastructure choice, not an assumed capability. |
| Audit integrity | Transactional append-only application records, restricted DB roles, backups and separately retained checkpoints/exports. Hashes or a DB hash chain alone cannot resist an administrator rewriting history. Add strong external immutability where required. |
| Automation | Prefill, route, verify, hash, produce receipts and prepare evidence. Discretionary decisions remain with appointed people unless explicitly delegated under a separate bounded policy. |

## X1 integration

First expose a **native human route**: product and security/data decisions occur in the register and the intake receives verified references. Human decisions do not require machine delegation POL-004. Keep that standing-delegation route separate: it needs real POL-004 approval and the four distinct machine identities specified by the current orchestrator.

Do not put database writes in the existing CommonJS adapter, which expressly forbids them. Native actions own mutations. An authenticated read API or read-only resolver exposes committed records to evidence validation. Later automated package creation requires an explicitly reviewed contract and authorization path, not a silent alteration of the old contract.

| Existing intake field | Register output |
| --- | --- |
| Product decision | Actual choice; map APPROVED/REJECTED/REVISION_REQUESTED to APPROVE/REJECT/REVISE. |
| Product approval reference | Persisted product decision record ID. |
| Product approval timestamp | Verified decision time, ISO-8601 with timezone. |
| Product signature reference | Native confirmation verification-event ID or validated external signature reference. |
| Evidence decision | Actual security/data decision. |
| Credential approval reference | Record explicitly authorizing the exact credential reference and evidence use. |
| Seeded-data approval reference | Record explicitly authorizing fixture tenants/data scope. |
| Evidence approval timestamp | Verified time of the complete authorization. For split decisions preserve underlying times and gate against the latest required one. |
| Evidence signature reference | Receipt covering required security/data scopes. Split signatures remain separately discoverable. |

One security record can supply credential and seeded-data references only if verified authority and explicit decision cover both. For separately signed records, export a verifiable package linking constituent decisions; its ID is not a new signature. Expand the intake contract where needed instead of forcing multiple signatures into one invented string. The same permanent URI may appear in several source columns when it exposes each value and verification evidence.

Update preflight/validation to resolve trusted source, exact revision, real actor mapping, scoped appointments, expiry and revocation. Freeze a current source bundle at submission: September 2 measurement changes mean an old manifest's self-hash does not show its referenced files are current.

After valid product/security approvals: prepare a new run, collect the 42 route/locale/viewport observations after the latest required approval, bind artifacts to build/fixture/measurement versions, assess remaining performance signals, and obtain independent technical review. Zero rows can be valid when reconciled with authorized fixture, date filter and permissions; a loading placeholder is not empty-data proof. Product approval sets budgets, not proof they passed. POL-003 delegates gate review; it is not the technical pass. Production release remains separate.

## Quality risks and meaningful acceptance checks

| Scenario | Required verification |
| --- | --- |
| Cross-tenant access / substituted actor | Reject guessed record IDs, foreign artifacts and client actor/organization overrides. |
| Unappointed, expired or conflicting approver | Fail with precise authority reason; check limits and live authority at consumption. |
| Authentication without decision confirmation | No approval. Test challenge binding and stale, future or replayed auth evidence. |
| File or request changed after review | Reject stale revision/challenge; preserve immutable old version and require a fresh decision. |
| Partial writes, double-click, racing revocation | Failure injection proves atomic decision/event/challenge writes and deterministic retries; revoke prevents later consumption. |
| Missing upload or scan-only signature | Keep unverified with named missing check. Uploading a scan cannot mark it verified. |
| Storage loss / PDF failure | Track decision and artifact availability separately; gate requires necessary evidence. Retry exports safely. |
| Simulator or manually completed legacy form | Reject non-authoritative source and unverifiable IDs regardless of structural completeness. |
| UI errors / accessibility | Verify EN/FR, keyboard/focus, confirmation summary, access-denied states and recovery. Never display approved optimistically before commitment. |
| Stale gate report | Include evaluation time and exact revisions; repeat live checks before action. |
| Growth / retention | Index organization/status/assignee/date; cursor pagination; lazy artifacts; policy-based archival and restore drill. No unbounded history responses. |

## Build order and completion gates

1. **Register foundation.** Scoped schema/migration, mandatory audit, permissions, private artifact adapter, read service and append-only decisions. Tenant isolation and transaction tests unlock safe drafts and retrieval.
2. **Establishment and authorities.** Setup charter, verified owner binding, appointments and activation. Exercise with explicitly non-authoritative test identities. Real activation requires the owner's information and acts.
3. **Human approval.** Inbox, exact-version review, password confirmation, receipts, protected links, expiry/revocation and exports. Verify approve/reject/revise and paper-import pending states.
4. **X1 consumer.** Native resolver/intake export; bind current contracts, identities and evidence. Prove authentic valid records satisfy their own conditions, while expired or mismatched ones do not advance X1.
5. **Broader reuse.** Governance/change templates and references from existing domain approvals. Execution and standing delegation require separate bounded work with existing controls.

Pilot in non-production behind a governance-specific flag. Treat governance access as a core administrative capability so setup does not depend on inventory entitlements. Rollback disables new decisions and restores the prior application while retaining readable register history. No destructive down migration. No timeline or production readiness claim without implementation and verification.

Chosen defaults fit the current stack. Activation details remain intentionally blank: actual owning organization, owner identity/authority basis, named approvers/verifier, adoption of signing method, validity/retention choices and hosting/storage details. ## Out of scope

Automatic authority creation, fabricated signatures, arbitrary hard-gate waivers, public evidence links, replacing financial controls with generic approval, production deployment, signature-provider procurement, and legal-equivalence claims about signature methods.

After blueprint confirmation, the next deliverable is implementation, not another prerequisite approval system.
